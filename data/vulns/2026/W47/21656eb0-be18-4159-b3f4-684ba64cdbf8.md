# In the Linux kernel, the following vulnerability has been resolved\: can\: protect the receive lists with a per\-device lock The receive l\.\.\.

- **ID**: CVE\-2026\-99672
- **危険度**: High（重要） (CVSS 7.8)
- **OS**: Linux
- **公開**: 2026-11-20

## 原文

In the Linux kernel, the following vulnerability has been resolved\:
  
can\: protect the receive lists with a per\-device lock
  
The receive lists of a CAN device are located in the device's  
can\_ml\_priv and travel with the device when it is moved to another  
network namespace\. But they are protected by the per\-netns  
rcvlists\_lock\. So can\_rx\_unregister\(\) needs dev\_net\(dev\) to pick the  
right lock and silently returns when the passed namespace does not  
match dev\_net\(dev\) anymore\.
  
isotp\_release\(\) and bcm\_release\(\) leave their notifier list before they  
take any lock that serializes against the netdev notifier, which runs  
under RTNL\. When the bound device is moved to another namespace in this  
window, the notifier skips the socket and the release path has to remove  
the filters itself\. isotp\_release\(\) passes the socket's namespace, which  
does not match dev\_net\(dev\) anymore\. bcm\_rx\_unreg\(\) passes dev\_net\(dev\),  
which can\_rx\_unregister\(\) reads again for its check, so a namespace  
change between both reads makes the check fail as well\. In both cases  
can\_rx\_unregister\(\) silently returns\. As can\_rx\_register\(\) stores  
rcv\-&gt;sk without a reference, the remaining receivers point to the freed  
socket \(isotp\) or the freed op \(bcm\)\:
  
  BUG\: KASAN\: use\-after\-free in isotp\_rcv\+0x1570/0x24d0  
  Read of size 1 at addr ffff888118130552 by task isotp\_ns\_uaf/578  
   can\_rcv\_filter\+0x4af/0x8c0  
   can\_receive\+0x28d/0x3c0  
   can\_rcv\+0x2a9/0x310  
   \_\_netif\_receive\_skb\_one\_core\+0x21a/0x260  
   process\_backlog\+0x210/0x760
  
Besides this, the lists of a device are protected by the rcvlists\_lock  
of the namespace that is passed in\. Callers which pass dev\_net\(dev\) read  
it at different times, so the same lists may be modified under the locks  
of different namespaces while the device is moved\.
  
Taking RTNL in these release paths is no option\: raw\_bind\(\),  
raw\_setsockopt\(\) and raw\_release\(\) already hold RTNL while adding and  
removing up to CAN\_RAW\_FILTER\_MAX filters per socket, which unprivileged  
users can trigger repeatedly and which makes RTNL a global bottleneck\.
  
Instead fix the root cause and add a spinlock to struct  
can\_dev\_rcv\_lists, which protects the receive lists of a CAN device or  
the 'any' CAN device lists of a namespace\. It is initialized in  
can\_set\_ml\_priv\(\), which is used by all CAN network devices\. With this,  
can\_rx\_unregister\(\) finds the subscription independently of the  
current namespace of the device\. The protocols keep the device valid  
with their tracked references\. The per\-netns rcvlists\_lock only protects  
the receive list statistics anymore, and CAN\_RAW, CAN\_BCM and CAN\_ISOTP  
pass the namespace that has been used for can\_rx\_register\(\)\.
  
So isotp\_release\(\) and bcm\_release\(\) now remove their filters correctly  
without RTNL, and raw\_release\(\), raw\_setsockopt\(\), raw\_bind\(\) and  
isotp\_bind\(\) do not need RTNL anymore and add and remove their filters  
under lock\_sock\(\)\. To serialize bind\(\) with NETDEV\_UNREGISTER, bind\(\)  
publishes the device inside the RCU section of its lookup, and  
raw\_notify\(\) and isotp\_notify\(\) recheck the bound device under lock\_sock\(\)\.  
As RTNL does not keep NETDEV\_DOWN out of bind\(\) anymore, bind\(\) checks  
IFF\_UP after publishing the bound device, with a barrier pair against  
raw\_notify\(\) and isotp\_notify\(\), so a down transition during bind\(\) is  
still reported with ENETDOWN\.
